# Mixed-swap power protection, gameplay review and 900-coin life refill

24 October 2026. Unity remains 2022.3.62f3; the live board is M46/M48 and the app flow remains HomeLevelFlow with M67 celebrations. This review follows source71. Existing artwork, sprite GUIDs, level layouts, portrait policy, Shop containment and checkpoint65/66/67 repairs are preserved.

## Power-up rule correction

Checkpoint25 deliberately allowed a mixed-swap reward to be hit and chained by the swapped source. The owner's latest request supersedes that behavior. `M46/Runtime/BoardModel.cs` now reserves the IDs of powers earned by the initial mixed gem/power match. The swapped power still activates at its swapped position, and pre-existing powers hit by its attack still chain. The newly earned power survives that originating wave, including its already-existing chained powers. `M46/Runtime/LivePowerPlan.cs` also excludes reserved rewards from aimed projectile targets.

The reservation is transient and ends before gravity and subsequent cascade resolution. It does not create permanent invulnerability, change the checkpoint format, invent a Golden+Golden rule or merge Rocket and Bomb. A later action can activate the earned item normally. Original blast artwork may pass across the protected cell; the reward is neither consumed nor queued by that wave.

Fresh native verification passes 4,113 checks over 360 mixed-swap cases, covering all five match recipes, six source powers, rotations, both drag directions and three board sizes. All 360 rewards survive; 240 cases include actual source contact with the reward cell. Four rotated existing-chain controls still activate their pre-existing Bomb. Actual seeker targeting, checkpoint restoration, manual activation and a later independent Bomb hit are checked. `GameplayAuditTouchProbe.cs` now asserts the new rule and the original source action separately rather than expecting a chained newborn.

`LifeRules.RefillCoins` in `App/Profile/Runtime/PlayerProfile.cs` is now **900**. The existing zero-life-only full-refill rule is preserved. The native modal already derives its price, affordability and debit from this authority; no second hardcoded UI price was introduced.

## Smoothness findings and verification

The current accepted swap is scheduled at 120 ms and invalid out-and-back movement at 220 ms. One-cell gravity is 141 ms; section feed spacing is 35 ms. Matched gems release at 90 ms. The checkpoint67 fixes still allow landing squash, break fragments and collection tails to continue independently while subsequent board work progresses. The current review does not blindly shorten authored effect clips or obstacle clearance clocks.

A separate onset-clock defect was confirmed in `M47/Runtime/GameplayScreen.cs`. Its Update advances playback before reading a new command. Model solving and queue/resource preparation can then occupy much of that frame before Render exposes the animation's first pose. The next frame's delta includes that earlier work. Even with the existing 40 ms swap advance fence, a heavy command could consume animation time that had never been visible, shortening a scheduled 120 ms swap and sometimes skipping its first pose in externally driven probes.

Newly enqueued commands now wait for their first actual Render. That Render records a wall-clock anchor only when a playback step is present. The following advance discounts pre-presentation work once, using the existing scaled time and playback speed; subsequent phases retain their original clocks. Resource waits with no current step cannot consume the anchor, and Load clears it. This preserves checkpoint67's continuous cascades, independent falling/landing/effects, pause behavior and authored timings. It does **not** remove synchronous command latency or certify that every hitch is resolved.

The new `PlaybackOnsetVerification` suite passes **259 native Play Mode checks across eight cases**. These are different puzzles, but the current delay is independently visible and cannot be described as resolved.

`BoardModel.Settle` detects subsequent matches after complete gravity; `BoardPlayback.Fall` assigns one phase the longest route contact; `GameplayScreen.Next` advances at that boundary. Body sampling owns one current snapshot, while history carries effects and landing tails rather than arbitrary unfinished routes. Consequently, zero extra scheduled delay after the **global final contact** does not imply immediate reaction at an earlier **local contact**. The first-pose repair is separate from this gap.
