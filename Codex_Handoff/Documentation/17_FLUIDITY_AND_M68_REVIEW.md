# Gameplay fluidity, navigation and M68 integration

20 October 2026 · Unity 2022.3.62f3 · current approved timing update at the end; earlier M68 evidence retained below

This follows the APK playback/UI review in `16_PLAYBACK_AND_UI_REVIEW.md`. The owner requested faster interaction, concurrent power/refill presentation, fixed navigation, the supplied M68 celebration, and a return to Home with reward animation. Uploaded documents were read as asset specifications and historical evidence, not as additional user commands.

## Repairs

- **Move search:** `BoardModel.LegalSwaps` checks runs/squares touching the two exchanged pieces instead of rebuilding every match group for every possible swap. The native comparison verifies the same ordered legal moves on all 50 openings; deterministic campaign replay checks remain separate.
- **Input:** a drag commits after moving a quarter of a cell from its actual pointer-down position. It no longer waits for finger-up. A power activates on one tap. The existing pointer ownership/multitouch cancellation gate remains in use.
- **Loading:** a swap starts without waiting for the whole cascade's texture dependencies. Common refill/match textures are prepared during level initialization; subsequent dependencies are prefetched, with shared asynchronous requests and a readiness gate at the step that needs them. Initialization and cold asset loading remain measurable work.
- **Power/refill scheduling:** the model retains its deterministic resolution order and rules. A presentation schedule starts hit powers at their contact times. Independent tracks overlap; repeated damage on the same obstacle and descendant triggers retain causal order. Each column refills once its cell/carrier reservations release, while fragments and other visual tails continue. Cabinet reservations cover both columns for the full clear, and goal flights arrive before completion. Powers elsewhere on the board are not automatically consumed. Golden+Golden remains undefined.
- **Sampling:** the live renderer reuses draw/body/goal buffers, caches atlas lookup keys, avoids unchanged HUD/renderer updates, and computes history expiry once per event. Ordinary sprites use native automatic texture binding; custom property blocks are limited to active glow. A native pixel comparison verifies identical output after clearing a glow block on the same sprite. Expired effects no longer incur a minimum 30-second scan. Snapshot-returning review APIs preserve independent output ownership.
- **Navigation:** every page uses Home's established dock width, safe-area inset and height; other pages reserve the same space above their scroll content. Press/release feedback animates artwork while keeping hit rectangles fixed. Reduced motion retains tint feedback.
- **Completion:** rewards/progression still settle in `LevelCompleteSession`. Celebration/finale closure returns to interactive Home, where earned coins and chapter progress animate. Closure never auto-opens the next-level preview or Journey reader. Animation callbacks do not grant rewards.

## M68 assets

Both supplied archives were CRC/read checked and their Unity payloads compared. One asset identity set was imported under `Assets/HeartOfTheRain/M68Celebration`, retaining GUIDs. Authoring scripts and document prompts were not executed.

The delivered blink PNG is truncated inside its IDAT stream and lacks the remaining image data/end marker. The repair reproduces the supplied eye-mask/export arithmetic using the supplied master and blink source. All **1,136 complete surviving rows** match exactly, with zero channel differences. The original remains in the local delivery cache; `M68_Blink_Repair.json` records both hashes and the retained GUID. Unity importer settings are applied through the pinned Editor API so the authored dimensions are retained.

The runtime uses the authored 5,070-vertex mesh, 210 position samples, original master/blink textures and effect timeline. It does not load the 14 reference atlas pages into gameplay. The legacy cutout rig is replaced in current level/chapter presentation; its original assets remain for provenance. The new graphic explicitly requires a CanvasRenderer. The shader blends the supplied blink texture without scene lighting or an extra character fade.

Level playback uses elapsed unscaled time, the supplied square layout and 96% maximum scrim. The new cue starts once at 0.18 seconds with gain 0.60 and respects SFX settings. Tap-to-skip fades out; background time does not advance the clip. Reduced motion uses the supplied still. Chapter dialogue holds the authored character pose instead of looping the level clip.

Six native 768-pixel compositions at 0.20/0.65/1.15/1.55/1.88/2.75 seconds were compared with the supplied baked frames. Normalized full-image RGBA error is approximately 0.18–0.24%, within the 1% regression threshold. Rasterization/filter differences remain; this is not a new art approval. `Verification/Evidence/Fluidity/M68/Comparison.json` and the paired PNGs contain the native results.

The actual APK GUID-payload audit (`Build_Asset_Inclusion.json`) confirms the five runtime textures and excludes all 14 atlas pages. The Editor build report did not expose packed source paths after Play Mode; that unavailable observation is retained separately and is superseded by the APK audit. Build inclusion is not a runtime residency measurement.

### Findings in priority order

| Priority | Shortcoming in Heart of the Rain | Recorded comparison and source explanation | Next correction and acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | A hit power-up waits through its normal activation timeline. | In the paired blast-to-rocket recordings, Match Villains' blast begins around 0.750 s and rocket travel is visible by 0.833–0.917 s. Our blast begins around 2.333–2.417 s; the rocket remains in place through the next frames and fires around 3.333–3.500 s. These are approximate video landmarks, sampled at 12 fps. `BoardModel.Hit` queues the child at contact, but `LivePowerPlan.Build` gives it the same plan as a direct tap. `ReviewPlan` sets a standalone rocket's release to 1.0 s, with charge only in its last 0.22 s. The earlier concurrency repair starts the child track at contact without removing this internal wait. | Use a short, visibly acknowledged ignition for hit powers; tune direct-tap anticipation separately. Verify actual contact-to-ignition and tap-to-ignition, not only queue entry. Retain one activation per power, approved targets, model damage order and obstacle reservations. |
| P0 | Refill is still coordinated too broadly. | Match Villains' columns refill during blasts, projectile travel and continuing chains. Our blast-to-rocket case does overlap refill in some columns, but the source column remains held and replacements arrive in batches. `BoardPlayback` retains the latest reservation for an entire column, including power end times and hits in other segments. Every motion in that column receives that delay. An unaffected segment separated by an intact blocker can therefore wait for unrelated work. | Schedule release by occupied cell/connected falling segment and actual carrier clearance, then derive each piece's dependencies. Preserve multi-cell obstacle footprints and avoid dropping pieces through an active carrier. Record split columns, several hits in one column and mixed falling powers. |
| P1 | Falls have little sense of acceleration or weight. | Live gravity uses 0.30 s for every distance. `BoardPresentation.SampleLive` uses cubic ease-out: `1-(1-u)^3`, which starts fastest and slows throughout the fall. Ignoring the small positional ripple, it completes about 70% of travel in the first 100 ms and only 3.7% in the last 100 ms. Long drops start much faster than short drops, yet land simultaneously. The recordings show the resulting slide/stop rhythm beside Match Villains' more continuous motion. | Use distance-aware fall times, a controlled acceleration/terminal-speed profile, and a brief landing response. Review one fall component with the owner before changing its look. Verify one-, three- and long-cell falls and pails without changing final cells or RNG. |
| P1 | Incoming tiles form a synchronized train. | `BoardModel.Gravity` creates ordered spawn positions; the live scheduler assigns identical start and duration to every incoming tile in a column. No spawn stagger is added. The earlier offline `ReviewPlan` contains distance-dependent timing and an 18 ms incoming stagger, but that branch is skipped for live input. Old review footage therefore does not prove the live board has those refinements. | Give incoming pieces spacing and arrival times consistent with the surviving stack, and add a restrained stagger where needed. Verify gaps, board holes, blocked spawn segments and no overlaps at arrival. |
| P1 | A stable part of the board cannot accept another gesture while any turn work remains. | `InteractionGate` rejects input whenever `Busy` is true; `ReadPointer` cancels the pointer. Busy includes the active presentation and loading gates. Power-step duration also includes goal-collection arrivals. The source contains no pending-gesture buffer. A player swiping near the end of a cascade can receive no action. Match Villains' input policy during cascades was not tested, so this is a confirmed source limitation rather than a claimed competitor capability. | First distinguish board instability from decorative tails. Consider one validated buffered gesture with visible acknowledgement; revalidate after settlement. Keep wins, goals, retries and settlement correct. Concurrent model actions would require a separate design decision. |
| P2 | Invalid swaps lack a visible swap-and-return response. | `BoardModel.Swap` restores the exchange and rejects without enqueuing a presentation step when no match exists. `GameplayScreen.Accept` gives an error hint/sound. This source finding was not timed as a successful native comparison. The preliminary Match Villains red/red swipe was rejected and is excluded from accepted-match evidence. | Add a short reversible visual response without spending a move or mutating the settled model. Verify that it cannot double-commit or conflict with a following gesture. |

The paired recordings confirm that remaining sluggishness includes authored presentation waits, not just frame stalls. The previous 28 ms first-step observation measures the `StepStarted` event. It does **not** mean the rocket ignites or damages its first target in 28 ms. Future verification must track visible acknowledgement, ignition, first vacancy, first refill motion, landing and input availability separately. Heavy-combo CPU/GPU spikes remain the separate limitation already recorded above.

The first corrections should be the hit-power ignition path and live fall/refill scheduler. A global playback-speed increase would also shorten effects and goal flights, while retaining the same synchronized motion and overly broad reservations; it would not address these causes.

The comparison temporarily used a 900 × 1600 display override and restarted our app to obtain a stable portrait layout. The immediate capture during resize shows a stretched old frame and is excluded from steady-state layout conclusions. The override was reset afterward, restoring the original physical size. Orientation policy and resize recovery need a separate device/UI decision; no orientation setting in the project was changed. The updated APK is verified on original instance 0, following the owner's installation request; the preceding delivery's instance-offline record is archived separately.

Scope: four accepted Match Villains actions used existing on-board powers and a natural match, taking the existing in-progress board from five moves to one. No purchase, premium tool, retry, app-data clear or account operation was used. Our pre-level items were selected from the existing session inventory. An early automated setup left the pre-level modal open; that clip is explicitly excluded. `Review.json` distinguishes useful captures from preliminary/rejected attempts. Recordings may perturb frame timing; this review does not supply new performance percentiles, audio/haptic approval, physical-device certification, exhaustive competitor coverage or approval to copy competitor artwork/mechanics.

## Approved reaction, gravity and input refinements — 20 October 2026

The owner approved the comparison recommendations. This pass changes the live presentation and input response while retaining the model's operations, targeting, snapshots, level masks, RNG and rewards. Rocket/Bomb remain separate and Golden+Golden remains undefined. No artwork, existing asset GUID, engine or SDK was replaced.

- **Ignition:** each presentation track has a local anticipation offset. Direct rockets/bombs release at 160 ms and hit rockets/bombs at 100 ms after their track starts. Orb channel and seeker takeoff anticipation are also shortened. Child start times are mapped from the parent's actual presentation contact, including causality delays from shared obstacles. The model's original plans and damage order are not rewritten. Combined-power merge choreography remains intact. Visuals and audio use the same track offset; future tracks cannot draw before their contact.
- **Gravity:** live falls accelerate from rest, with a terminal speed for long drops. Travel takes approximately 141/245/461 ms for one/three/nine cells, followed by a restrained 70 ms landing response. Incoming pieces are staggered by 18 ms within their falling section. Audio follows actual arrivals. Full settling time can increase for large drops; it is no longer forced to 300 ms for every distance.
- **Refill:** reservations are attached to cells and obstacle footprints. Each motion waits for the cells on its path and the piece ahead in the same connected section; a hit below an intact blocker or gap does not hold an independent upper section. Cabinet clear occupancy and goal arrival requirements remain enforced. The level 42 case exposed virtual incoming pieces appearing in an upper island before reaching their own lower spawn portal; those pieces are now hidden above their own entry boundary.
- **Late gestures:** once the final board positions are stable, one gesture may be buffered through the remaining settling/collection tail. It is visibly acknowledged using the existing focus artwork. The same board instance, turn and piece IDs are checked before execution. Pausing, focus loss, modal ownership, disabling and reloading clear the intent. Early gestures during moving cascades remain blocked; concurrent model turns were not introduced.
- **Invalid swaps:** an authored no-match swap now visibly moves out and returns in 220 ms. It leaves the model unchanged, spends no move, and emits neither an accepted action nor a turn-settled notification. Pause freezes that presentation normally.

Changed runtime files: `M46/Runtime/BoardPlayback.cs`, `BoardPresentation.cs`, the `Motion` presentation fields in `BoardModel.cs`, `M47/Runtime/GameplayScreen.cs`, `FeedbackPlanPure.cs`, and the validated gesture buffer in `M48/Runtime/ReviewLayoutPure.cs`.
